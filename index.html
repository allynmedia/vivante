Paid social: the summer told us where to spend

Verified against the live Meta ad account connected in AgencyAnalytics, June 1–August 31, 2026.

Three months of testing produced two clear, repeatable patterns rather than one lucky month — the basis for how September's larger budget is structured.

Removing zip-code restrictions cut cost per lead roughly 3x

$5.34 per lead once geographic restrictions were lifted and ad copy carried the targeting, against $16.73 per lead on the more geographically restricted campaign.

Specific, sensory creative consistently outperformed general messaging

Posts built around a concrete moment — coffee on the balcony, a shared meal — outperformed broader lifestyle or FAQ-style content across the summer.

Dining and wellness imagery is the strongest visual territory

This creative direction reached people roughly 24% more efficiently than broader lifestyle imagery, and is the foundation for September's new video.

Worth watching: the two lead-generation campaigns carried the summer's volume, but the awareness campaign alone reached over 220,000 people on about $1,240 — an efficient top of the funnel that September's structure now intentionally builds on rather than lets run unmanaged.
The fuller demand picture: Meta's 276 native-form leads are not the only signal of intent Turtle Creek captured this summer. The website's own "Turtle Creek Interest" form — a separate, direct channel — logged 60 additional submissions from June through August (16, then 22, then 22 per month, holding steady even as Meta lead volume tapered). Counted together, Turtle Creek generated 336 pieces of qualified interest this summer across its two working channels.

Organic & SEO: a small but growing footprint

Semrush position tracking, vivanteliving.com, U.S. database, monthly data March–August 2026.

Turtle Creek's own page is gaining organic ground slowly, off a small base — consistent with a pre-opening property that has not yet built a dedicated content presence.

Ranking keyword count on the Turtle Creek page is up 37% since March

From 169 ranking keywords in March to 232 in August, with estimated organic traffic to the page rising from 76 to 92 visits per month over the same period.

Sitewide traffic is up even as the sitewide keyword count is down

Across all of vivanteliving.com, Semrush-estimated organic traffic rose from roughly 2,880 to 4,020 visits per month between March and August, while the number of ranking keywords fell from 2,160 to 1,612 over the same window.

What this needs before we present it as a clean win: a traffic increase alongside a shrinking keyword footprint usually means the site is consolidating onto fewer, higher-intent terms — but it can also mean lower-value long-tail rankings are dropping off. This is worth a short site-auditor pass before it goes into a client-facing SEO narrative, and it sits outside Turtle Creek's own page performance, which is genuinely growing.

What Google itself says: first-party search demand

Google Search Console, vivanteliving.com/turtle-creek-new/, June 1–August 31, 2026. This is Google's own measured data, not a third-party estimate.

Search Console confirms the Semrush trend with harder numbers, and surfaces exactly which high-volume terms are close to converting into real traffic.

The page is being shown, but not yet earning its share of clicks

33,372 impressions and 421 clicks over three months, for a 1.26% click-through rate at an average position of 17 — solidly on page two, where impressions accumulate but clicks are scarce.

Brand terms are already winning; category terms are the open opportunity

"Vivante turtle creek" sits at position 1.5 and converts well. The real upside is category volume the page hasn't captured yet: "assisted living dallas" drew 1,982 impressions at position 13 but only 4 clicks, and "luxury senior living dallas" sits at position 7 on 889 impressions — one content push away from page one.

What this means for scope: these are exactly the kind of gaps a dedicated SEO engagement would target with supporting content and internal linking. Right now they sit outside Allyn's confirmed scope for Turtle Creek — worth having in hand for the Nexus conversation, framed as opportunity cost of leaving search unmanaged, not as work already underway.

Newport Google Ads: retiring the legacy account structure

Newport Mesa & Newport Center only. Turtle Creek has no Google Ads presence and isn't part of this cleanup.

The Newport Google account carried years of prior setup that needed active retirement, not just monitoring. That work is underway and already visible in the account structure.

Legacy campaigns are being wound down, not left to run alongside new ones

Campaigns dating back to 2022 under the prior "ELK" naming convention are being phased out — one active campaign is labeled exactly that, "08/2026 - Phase out" — while newly structured campaigns under Allyn's naming and tracking standards have taken their place, including a new PerfMax website-traffic campaign launched in August.

The lead-quality issue traced to the old setup is diagnosed and being addressed

Calls from clearly misaligned audiences — people searching for housing assistance rather than luxury senior living — were traced most likely to broad-match keyword bleed and PMax placement behavior inherited from the prior account structure. Account-level negative keywords (poverty, homeless, section eight, affordable) have been added to close that gap going forward.

Working directly with Google to prevent a repeat

Allyn has been in direct contact with the Newport account's Google representative to confirm the account is clear of the prior setup's issues and to flag anything that could resurface the same problem.

Why this belongs in the report: account hygiene doesn't show up in a spend or conversion number, but it's the difference between Newport's budget reaching genuine prospective residents and their families versus leaking to unrelated search traffic. This is foundational work, and it's now largely behind us rather than an open risk.

The wider Vivante program, for context

Newport Mesa and Newport Center run on a separate ad account and separate objective; shown here only to frame where Turtle Creek sits in the total account.

Google Ads activity is entirely on the Newport side today

Newport Mesa and Newport Center carried roughly $48,800 in Google Ads spend from June 1 through September 3. Turtle Creek has no Google or Bing campaigns currently running under this account — search remains a gap, not a channel that is underperforming.

Sitewide conversions rose even as sitewide sessions fell in August

Across all three properties on GA4, sessions dropped from 11,646 in July to 7,803 in August, while recorded conversions rose from 270 to 386. That is a large enough swing that it is worth confirming it reflects genuine lead quality rather than a change in what GA4 is counting as a conversion, before it is presented as a win.

Bing Ads went live in August, with spend but no recorded conversions yet

The Newport Bing account spent $2,233 across 694 clicks and 237,000 impressions in its first month, with zero conversions recorded. Worth a conversion-tracking check before drawing any performance conclusion — this reads as a setup gap, not a Bing performance problem.

Why this matters for Turtle Creek specifically: the search gap is the clearest opening in the account. Turtle Creek currently earns organic visibility only through its single landing page and has no paid search presence at all, at the exact moment the sales office is opening and search intent for the address is about to become far more valuable.
No phone call volume attributed to Turtle Creek in August: a CallRail search across the account's tracked calls turned up zero calls tied to a Turtle Creek landing page for the month. Given the sales office opening, it is worth confirming Turtle Creek's tracking numbers are actually live on the site rather than assuming there is simply no call demand yet.

What this shapes for the month ahead

  • 1

    Carry the broad-targeting, sensory-creative pattern into September at $2,000/month

    The four-campaign structure — awareness, traffic, retargeting, boosted posts — already reflects what the summer proved worked. The main discipline for September is protecting that pattern as spend triples, not redesigning it.

  • 2

    Confirm the August GA4 conversion swing before it appears in client reporting

    A 43% rise in conversions on 33% fewer sessions is a strong claim either way. Worth five minutes in GA4's event configuration before it is framed as a quality improvement.

  • 3

    Treat search as the open item in the Turtle Creek scope conversation

    The only executed agreement in view for Turtle Creek covers paid and organic social; the expanded scope that would add Google, Bing, and SEO management for all three properties, referenced here as the Nexus Expanded Services agreement, exists as an April 2026 proposal. Confirm its current signature status before this report is used to imply that search is already inside Allyn's managed scope for Turtle Creek.

  • 4

    Give the new Newport campaigns a full month before calling the ELK cleanup proven

    The replacement campaigns are live, but a phase-out and a rebuild need at least one clean reporting month before their lead quality can be compared fairly against the old structure. Worth a specific before/after readout in October rather than a general "it's fixed" claim.

  • 5

    Confirm Turtle Creek's call tracking number is live before the sales office opens

    Zero attributed calls in August could mean genuinely no call demand yet, or it could mean the tracking number on the Turtle Creek pages isn't swapping correctly. Worth a five-minute check rather than an assumption either way, especially with in-person interest about to rise.
